To the hiring team,

I build the layer most people would rather not touch: the part where the maths
stops being a diagram and has to run, in memory, on a real machine, without a
garbage collector to hide behind. I have spent more than twenty years at a
console, and my instinct when a problem is described to me is to reach for the
data structure and the invariant before I reach for a framework.

Most of my recent work is a single-writer resident field engine written in C.
It mmaps a fixed-offset packed store, serves queries over a Unix domain socket
with systemd socket activation and no libsystemd dependency, and folds new
information into the packed file in place — a range check and a bounded pwrite
under MAP_SHARED with an msync, rather than a serialize-and-swap. Concurrency is
handled by an advisory flock on a sidecar file whose owner string is checked
before any write; the kernel releasing that lock on process death is the entire
crash-recovery story. There is a leaky-integrator timer that decides when to
repack, charged by input size and bled with a time constant, firing at the knee
of its own charge curve rather than on a wall clock. None of that is exotic.
All of it is the result of asking, repeatedly, "what is the cheapest correct
thing here, and what is the invariant that lets me prove it is correct."

The mathematics side is hypercomplex algebra made executable: Cayley-Dickson
products up through the sedenions, zero-divisor structure, the Riemann
zeros as an addressing scheme, and a good deal of numerical linear algebra in
Python with NumPy where the shape of the array is the argument and the loop is
the bug. I am comfortable moving a hot path from Python into C, and more
comfortable still deleting the hot path because the representation was wrong.

My planning background is military PACE — primary, alternate, contingency,
emergency. I do not design to an expected case; I design so that every branch
has a defined fallback and every failure is explicit rather than silent. In
code that shows up as: exhaustive over sampled where the space is small enough
that exhaustive is exact; a guaranteed slow path behind every fast path;
functions that return a DEGENERATE sentinel instead of a confident wrong
number; and a control run before the measurement, never after.

I work in git from the command line, review my own diffs before anyone else
sees them, and write commit messages that a stranger could use to reconstruct
the reasoning. I document by writing the thing down cleanly, once, in prose,
with the provenance of every claim in the same sentence as the claim.

What I am looking for is a place where the hard part is real — a runtime, a
storage layer, a numerical kernel, a protocol — and where "it is correct
because here is the argument" is a sentence people want to hear rather than
tolerate. If that is the shape of the work, I would like to talk.

Thank you for your time.

Cody Michael Allison
