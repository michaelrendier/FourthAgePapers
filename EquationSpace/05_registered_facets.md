# EquationSpace test 4: do the facets of Σ_RB DERIVE their theories, or name them? (registered 2026-11-19, BEFORE running)

The module `ValaQuenta/modules/h_rb_hat` already carries the claim in the form of a table: σ = 2 → General Relativity, σ = 1 → Yang–Mills / Navier–Stokes (real projection), σ = ½ → Quantum Mechanics / Riemann zeros, σ < ½ → Fermat, with the operator Σ_RB = Σ_p p^{-σ}[R̂_p ⊗ ∂̂_∂M + ∂̂_∂M† ⊗ B̂_p], R̂ = xp (Berry–Keating), B̂ = ½p² + ℘(x; g₂, g₃). The question "can they be derived" asks whether anything in the code goes from the operator to the equation, or only attaches the equation's name to a σ.

**Predictions (written before the run):**
- **F1 (audit).** In each `facet_*` function the equation of the theory appears only as a string, and the numbers it computes are the coupling sums Σ_p p^{-σ} and constants: no facet function computes a quantity of the theory (a curvature, a gauge field strength, a Schrödinger spectrum) from the operator. Pass: for every facet, 0 computed quantities beyond coupling sums / constants / the Euler product.
- **F2 (R̂ is a boost generator).** The flow of the vector field x∂_x (the generator of R̂ = xp as a dilation) with flow-time t = ln p scales x by exactly p, and this is, to 10⁻¹², the same map as the Lorentz boost with Doppler factor p acting on one light-cone coordinate (engine `relativity`, `minkowski.lightcone_dilation_check`). Pass: residual < 1e-12 for p = 2, 3, 5, 7. (ESTABLISHED mathematics; what it shows is what R̂ generates, not that the sum over p is relativity.)
- **F3 (B̂ gives F = ma).** For B̂ = ½p² + V(x) with V the module's Weierstrass term, Hamilton's/Heisenberg's equation gives ṗ = −V′(x), i.e. m ẍ = F with F = −V′; a numerical trajectory under `blue_energy_weierstrass` satisfies ẍ = −V′(x) to 1e-6 relative. And the force family reachable this way is F = −℘′(x): a Newtonian 1/r potential is **not** in it (℘ has double poles, so V ~ x⁻²; the potential 1/r has a simple pole). Pass: both statements hold.
- **F4 (φ).** For the facet couplings σ ∈ {½, 1, 2}, no coupling sum, ζ(σ) or Euler product is within 0.1 % of φ, 1/φ, φ² or 1/φ². Pass: 0 hits. (If a hit appears it is reported with how many comparisons were made; with 4 targets and 3 σ the chance of a 0.1 % coincidence per comparison is small but counted.)
